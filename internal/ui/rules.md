# Texas Hold’em tournament reference

This is an original reference for players and operators covering core Texas Hold’em play and common tournament procedures. It is not an official or exhaustive rulebook. Home-game procedures, betting limits, and tournament-director rulings can differ. Agree on house rules before play and follow the tournament director’s ruling for disputes. This app records tournament information; it does not enforce betting or determine whether a player can cover a wager.

## Goal and cards

Texas Hold’em is played with a standard 52-card deck. Each player receives two private **hole cards**. Five shared **community cards** are dealt face up in three stages:

1. **Flop:** three cards.
2. **Turn:** one card.
3. **River:** one card.

Make the strongest five-card poker hand using any combination of your two hole cards and the five community cards. You may use both hole cards, one hole card, or no hole cards at all. If the five community cards make the best hand, every remaining player can use that same board hand.

## Hand rankings, strongest first

1. **Straight flush:** five consecutive ranks of one suit. An ace-high straight flush is a royal flush.
2. **Four of a kind:** four cards of one rank; fifth card is the kicker.
3. **Full house:** three of one rank and two of another. Compare the three-card rank first, then the pair.
4. **Flush:** five cards of one suit, not consecutive. Compare card ranks from highest to lowest.
5. **Straight:** five consecutive ranks in mixed or matching suits. Ace can be high (`10-J-Q-K-A`) or low (`A-2-3-4-5`), never wrap around (`K-A-2-3-4` is not a straight).
6. **Three of a kind:** three cards of one rank; compare the remaining kickers in order.
7. **Two pair:** compare the higher pair, then lower pair, then kicker.
8. **One pair:** compare the pair, then the three kickers in order.
9. **High card:** compare the five cards from highest to lowest.

Suits have no ranking in standard hand comparison. When two players have the same five-card rank and values, the pot is tied and split. A kicker matters only when it is part of the best five-card hand; unused hole cards do not break a tie.

## Betting-round outline

A hand normally proceeds through four betting rounds: pre-flop, flop, turn, and river. The two players designated by the button position post the small blind and big blind before cards are dealt. Antes, if used, are also posted before the hand according to the game’s rules.

When action reaches you, the legal choices depend on the previous action and the betting structure. Common choices are:

- **Check:** pass action without wagering when no bet is owed.
- **Bet:** make the first wager of a betting round.
- **Call:** match the current bet.
- **Raise:** increase the current bet, subject to the game’s minimum-raise rule and remaining chips.
- **Fold:** give up the hand. Folded players cannot win the pot, but their committed chips remain in it.
- **All-in:** wager all chips you have available. A short all-in does not necessarily reopen betting for players who already acted; follow the house’s betting rules.

Action proceeds clockwise. A player must act in turn. Verbal declarations and chips placed into the pot may bind a player under the house rules. Protect your cards, make actions clear, and ask the dealer or tournament director before acting if unsure.

## Betting structures and action

The tournament or house rules should state whether betting is **no-limit**, **pot-limit**, or **fixed-limit**. In no-limit, a player may wager up to the chips available to them. Pot-limit caps a raise by the current pot size under the applicable calculation. Fixed-limit uses specified bet and raise sizes. This clock does not select or enforce a betting structure.

A betting round starts with the first live player in the action order. To continue in the hand, a player generally must match the current wager, unless they are all-in for less. A raise must satisfy the minimum raise rule in force. A raise is normally at least as large as the previous full bet or raise; a short all-in may be an exception and may not reopen action to a player who already acted. Exact reopening and under-call rules are procedural details: use the tournament’s rules and ask the floor when uncertain.

Check is available only when no wager is owed. A player facing a wager may fold, call, or raise where permitted. A player may announce an action verbally, but the binding effect of announcements, chips placed forward, string bets, and out-of-turn action depends on the rules in force. State one clear action and amount; do not angle-shoot or rely on ambiguity.

## Button, blinds, and action order

The dealer button identifies the nominal dealer position and moves clockwise after each hand. In a standard multiway game, the player left of the button posts the small blind and the next player posts the big blind; action begins to the left of the big blind pre-flop. On later streets, action normally begins with the first live player left of the button. Heads-up play has a special button/blind arrangement: the button posts the small blind and acts first pre-flop, then acts last on later streets. Tournament rules cover dead buttons, missed blinds, seating changes, and table balancing.

Players must act in turn. A player who is unsure whose turn it is should ask the dealer rather than act out of turn. Blinds and antes are forced contributions, not optional bets. A big-blind ante is posted according to the event’s procedure; it is not automatically an unmatched wager to return.

## Showdown and split pots

If betting ends with more than one player, eligible hands are compared at showdown. Each player’s best five-card hand is used. The highest hand wins the pot. Equal hands split that pot; any indivisible chip is awarded according to the house rule, commonly by a specified seat/button order.

If one player remains after everyone else folds, that player wins without showing their cards, subject to the rules in force. At showdown, cards speak: the best five-card hand determines the result, not a player’s verbal description. Showdown order and whether a player must show or may muck are tournament procedures; follow the dealer or floor.

When one or more players are all-in for different amounts, the pot may be divided into a main pot and side pots. A player can win only pots for which they are eligible. Folded players’ contributions remain in the pot, but folded hands cannot win. A genuinely unmatched excess wager may be returned to the bettor; an ante is not a wager to be returned merely because it was not matched. Tournament procedures determine how the dealer handles these amounts.

## Irregularities and rulings

A misdeal, exposed card, incorrect number of cards, premature board card, out-of-turn action, or mistaken pot requires a dealer or floor ruling. Do not decide that a hand is dead, redeal a card, or award a pot based only on this reference. Protect your cards and chips, stop further action when an error is noticed, and call the tournament director promptly. Remedies depend on when the error is discovered and whether substantial action has occurred.

Cards and chips should remain visible and identifiable. Keep cards on the table, protect a live hand, do not transfer chips to another player, and do not take chips off the table during play. The floor can clarify chip denominations, pot splits, side-pot eligibility, and any irregularity.

## Tournament conduct

- Blinds and antes may increase according to the published structure. The tournament director should announce level changes and breaks.
- A player who is all-in remains eligible only for pots they funded. A player who folds is out of that hand, not automatically out of the tournament.
- Tournament elimination is determined when a player loses all tournament chips under the event’s procedures. Simultaneous eliminations and tied finishing positions follow tournament rules.
- Do not expose or discuss live hands, angle-shoot, deliberately delay play, or interfere with another player’s decision. Keep cards and chips visible and act in turn.
- Ask for a ruling promptly. Do not make a side agreement that changes prize distribution unless tournament rules and the director permit it.

The poker-clock app does **not** deduct blinds or antes, track individual stacks, evaluate live hands, or establish eliminations automatically. The operator records purchases, sit-outs, and busts separately. Chip-up reminders do not change the tournament’s chip total.
